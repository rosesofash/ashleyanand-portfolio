Wealthsimple Data Transparency

  • Case Study
  • App Redesign
  • Solo Project

Timeline: March - April 2026 Tools: Figma Methods: User flows, trade-off sliders, Single Ease Question, user testing

Overview

I redesigned how Wealthsimple users find and understand their legal documents, on both mobile and desktop.

Banking apps hold some of our most sensitive information, from our name and email to our social insurance number and tax records. Yet in Wealthsimple’s app, I couldn’t find the Terms of Use without a Google search.

The Problem

Before choosing a project, I checked how easy it was to reach the Terms of Service in apps like Uber, WhatsApp, and YouTube. Then I chose an app with higher stakes: a bank.

Mapping the flow in Wealthsimple showed how hard it was. A user checks Profile, Settings, Personal Information, Login & Security, and every other Settings tab, hitting three dead ends before Support finally sends them to Wealthsimple’s website.

Every tester asked whether the Terms of Use were in the app at all. They were, just very hard to find.

Some information Wealthsimple collects

Your Identity

Your name, address, phone, email, birthdate, and Social Insurance Number.

Your ID and selfie

Copies of your government-issued ID, given to Wealthsimple or one of its service providers.

Linked accounts

Transactions from all your bank accounts and credit cards when you connect them through Plaid or Flinks.

Social media

Your username, email, picture, and gender if you interact with Wealthsimple’s content on socials.

AI service providers

What you type into Wealthsimple’s AI features may be processed by outside AI companies.

Service providers

Your information may go to affiliates and outside companies for identity checks, security, fraud prevention, etc.

Exploring Ideas

Based on the research I’d done on user data, I finally came to a problem statement.

How might we give users more control over their data?

After ideating, I sketched some features based on this problem:

  • A security history showing every time account data was accessed
  • A preferences tab to control what optional data Wealthsimple can use
  • A Legal tab in Settings
  • Subtext explaining why Wealthsimple needs each piece of personal data
  • A “What’s new” card showing recent changes to the documents
  • Highlighting text to get a simpler explanation
  • A summary of the most important clauses

Choosing what to build

I gave Wealthsimple users trade-off sliders to rate which ideas mattered most to them. I ended up further iterating and incorporating:

  1. An easier way to access legal documents
  2. A summary of recent changes
  3. A legal text simplifier
  4. A preferences tab to customize what data Wealthsimple takes

Testing & Iterating

Using the Single Ease Question, testers rated how easy it was to find the Terms of Use in the original app. Scores ranged from 2 to 3 out of 7, where 1 is very hard.

Testing my mid-fidelity screens gave me three clear changes:

  • Legal felt more natural inside Settings.
  • Settings took too many taps to reach, so it belonged in the bottom navigation bar.
  • Links to the Terms of Use should be highlighted in Wealthsimple’s accent colour.

Final Design

The final changes I chose:

Legal tab where users expect it

Settings now lives in the bottom navigation bar, with new Legal and Preferences tabs inside it and highlighted links to the Terms of Use and Privacy Policy. On desktop, Legal sits in the account menu.

What’s changed card

A recent updates card at the top of the Privacy Policy means users don’t have to reread the whole document every time it changes.

Simplified legal language tool

Users can highlight a complicated section to see a plain-language summary, so they can quickly decide whether to read it in full. In the prototype, this is a preset summary demonstrating the concept.

Why we need your data

Personal information pages now explain why Wealthsimple keeps each detail, such as tax rules requiring records for 7 years.
