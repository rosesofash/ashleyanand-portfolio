A11yson: A Cognitive-First Accessibility Engine

  • Logo Design
  • Accessibility
  • Team Project

Timeline: 24 hours, Hackville 2026 Tools: Figma, Procreate Methods: User research, Ideation

Overview

Hackville 2026’s theme was accessibility, covering physical, cognitive, and other needs. In 24 hours, our team built A11yson, an AI-powered cognitive accessibility platform designed to make the web neuro-inclusive. It is a Chrome extension that changes how any website is presented without changing what it says.

We ideated together, and I designed the brand identity and logo and co-designed the website. We shipped a functional front end and back end with a team of 4 within 24 hours.

The Problem

The hackathon began with an opening ceremony, as most hackathons are wont to do. We were walked through the hackathon and what sponsors would be there while we sat there tapping our feet in eager anticipation of the theme drop. Finally, they gave it to us: Accessibility. As a team, we considered many ideas. We considered everything from anatomy and health-related apps to a Chrome extension, and chose the extension to try building something we’d never made before.

The web is described as universal, but in practice it assumes one way of seeing, reading, and processing information. Many websites meet accessibility standards on paper but still overwhelm people with dense layouts, harsh contrast, motion, and clutter. Most accessibility efforts fix one website at a time, but people experience the web continuously. Accessibility shouldn’t feel like a fix for the user. It should remove unnecessary friction, so everyone starts from the same baseline.

How might we present information to users according to their cognitive needs?

Branding

A11Y is shorthand for accessibility, so A11yson is pronounced “Allison”. I chose a chameleon as our mascot because it adapts to its surroundings, just like A11yson adapts the internet to each person. The logo is hand-drawn: I used Pinterest for ideation and Procreate for the sketches.

I built the brand around green as people tend to view green as a calm, natural, and positive colour. Also, when you think of a chameleon, you tend to think green! When deciding on shades, I initially intended a light logo to go on a darker background, as I’m used to “dark mode” on my devices. However, according to accessibility guidelines, dark text on a light background is most legible and least straining on the eyes.

Many judges, especially the design judges, told us how much they loved the logo!

The Product

Users take a short quiz about how they experience websites, and A11yson uses AI to suggest a profile. They can pick from four presets, or build their own:

  • ADHD: reduces distractions and visual competition to support focus.
  • Dyslexia: switches to the OpenDyslexic font with extra letter and line spacing.
  • Sensory: softens colours and contrast and limits visual intensity.
  • Clean: a minimal, distraction-free reading view.

Users can also turn on grayscale, hide or blur images, block ads, and use text-to-speech. An AI voice companion can summarize a page or answer questions about it.
